UK Platform Fee Principles to Remember:
  • Flat fee vs Percentage: Percentage fee platforms (Vanguard 0.15%, AJ Bell 0.25%, HL 0.45%) are cheap for portfolios under £30k-£50k. As wealth exceeds £60k-£100k, flat subscriptions (like Interactive Investor) or capped platforms (AJ Bell £42/yr share cap) become significantly cheaper.
  • Fund Trap: Hargreaves Lansdown charges £0 to trade funds, but their 0.45% custody fee on funds is uncapped (£450/yr for £100k; £900/yr for £200k).
  • Trading 212 Zero Fee: 100% free custody and zero dealing commission, but does not provide traditional mutual funds/OEICs, and cannot accept employer SIPP contributions.

How to Choose the Right UK Platform: Impartial Rules of Thumb

Key insights that save UK investors hundreds or thousands in lifetime charges

1. The Percentage vs Flat Fee Crossover

If you have less than £30,000 to £50,000, percentage fee brokers (like Vanguard at 0.15% or Trading 212 at 0%) are usually cheapest. But once your total portfolio crosses £60,000 to £100,000+, Interactive Investor's flat subscription fee (£143.88/yr) or AJ Bell's £42/yr share cap makes them dramatically cheaper than Hargreaves Lansdown's uncapped 0.45% fund fee.

2. Employer SIPP Contributions

If you run a UK Limited Company and plan to make director pension contributions directly from your business account (saving Corporation Tax and National Insurance), Vanguard UK and Trading 212 will not work because they do not accept employer payments. You must use a platform like AJ Bell, Interactive Investor, Hargreaves Lansdown, or Fidelity.

3. Overseas Trading & FX Drag

If you frequently trade US tech giants (Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft), beware that high foreign exchange fees (HL at 1.00%, ii at 0.99%, AJ Bell/Fidelity at 0.75%) can quickly eclipse all custody fees. Trading 212 charges only 0.15% FX, while Interactive Investor lets you hold US Dollars in multi-currency accounts to avoid double conversion.
